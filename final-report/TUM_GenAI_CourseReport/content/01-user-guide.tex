\section{What is Dishify?}
Dishify turns the ingredients you already have into recipes you can actually
cook tonight. Tell it what is in your pantry by typing, speaking, or
photographing your fridge, and it searches a corpus of roughly
2.2~million recipes for dishes that fit both your craving and your shelf.

Every suggestion answers two questions up front: \emph{why does this fit me},
and \emph{what am I missing}. Recipes that clash with a stored allergy or diet
are removed before you ever see them. Dishify is built for everyday home cooks
who own food but not a plan, and who lose more time to deciding than to cooking.

\section{Getting Started}
Dishify runs on the web today; a native iOS app is also implemented and can be
run locally from source (not yet on the App Store). Both clients share the same
account and pantry. The landing page is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:ug-landing-page}.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.78\textwidth]{./Ressourcen/user-guide/landing-page.png}
    \caption{The Dishify landing page.}
    \label{fig:ug-landing-page}
\end{figure}

Getting your first recipe suggestion takes four quick steps:

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1pt, topsep=3pt]
    \item \textbf{Open Dishify} at \url{http://167.233.165.44} (no
          installation; works in any modern browser).
    \item \textbf{Sign up} with a username, email, and password to create your
          account.
    \item \textbf{Set what to avoid} (allergies, diets, or ingredients you
          would rather skip) so Dishify never suggests around them.
    \item \textbf{Add what you have} under \emph{Cook}, then press
          \emph{Find my recipes}.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent
Within seconds, your pantry becomes a ranked list of recipes made for you.

\section{Features}

\subsection{Dietary, Allergy, and Restriction Filtering}
No more scanning every recipe for hidden allergens or off-limits ingredients.
Dishify does it for you. Choose from 72 tags across allergies, diets, religious
and ethical rules, medical needs, and ingredient exclusions
(Figure~\ref{fig:ug-preferences}). These are not hints: a recipe that conflicts
with your preferences is removed before it ever reaches your results, so you
never have to double-check. Set it once, and it applies to every search from
then on.

\subsection{Pantry-based Recipe Recommendation}
Tell Dishify what is already in your kitchen, down to the exact quantity and
unit, like 2~eggs (Figure~\ref{fig:ug-ingredients}). Quantity is optional but
useful: a recipe needing $500$\,g of pasta when you have $200$\,g counts as a
partial rather than a full match. Dishify searches roughly 2.2~million recipes
for ones that fit both your pantry and your mood. Craving something specific?
Add a short eating vibe like ``fast'' or ``warm and cheap'' and Dishify steers
the ranking accordingly.

\subsection{Voice Input and Photo Scanning}
Hands full while unpacking groceries? Say what you have out loud, and Dishify
adds it to the pantry with no typing required. Or skip manual entry: snap a photo
of your fridge or pantry and Dishify recognizes what is there
(Figure~\ref{fig:ug-multimodal}). Either way, detected items appear for you to
confirm before anything is searched, so a misheard word never silently changes
your results.

\subsection{Reading Your Results}
Every suggestion is ranked by a relevance score that blends how well the recipe
matches your craving with how much of it you can already cook from the pantry
(Figure~\ref{fig:ug-results}). At a glance you see which ingredients you already
have and which ones you would still need; no guesswork required. Each card also
carries a short \emph{why it fits} explanation grounded in the recipe itself.

\subsection{Recipe Detail}
Opening a card shows the full picture: matched and missing ingredients, why
Dishify picked the recipe, and clear directions
(Figure~\ref{fig:ug-recipe}). Source recipes are often terse; Dishify can expand
them into numbered, beginner-ready steps with per-step timings and an estimated
total, without inventing ingredients the recipe does not imply.

\section{Tips and Troubleshooting}

\begin{description}[itemsep=2pt, topsep=3pt, leftmargin=*, style=nextline]
    \item[Set your restrictions before your first search.] They are the one
          setting that changes what Dishify is allowed to show you.
    \item[List staples too.] Adding salt, oil, and pepper to the shelf
          noticeably shortens the ``NEED'' list on most cards.
    \item[Keep the craving short.] Two or three words (``quick'', ``warm'',
          ``cheap'') steer the search well; long sentences dilute it.
    \item[The first search of a session can be slower.] The search service
          loads its embedding model on first use; later searches are faster.
    \item[Explanations look plain?] If the language model is unreachable, or
          returns something that cannot be verified against the recipe, Dishify
          falls back to a factual matched/missing summary rather than showing
          an unchecked explanation. Results stay usable.
\end{description}
