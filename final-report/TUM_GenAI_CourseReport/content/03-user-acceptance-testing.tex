\chapter{User Acceptance Testing}
\label{ch:uat}

The purpose of the User Acceptance Testing was to determine whether Dishify
solves a relevant problem for its intended users and whether the application is
understandable, useful, and trustworthy in realistic everyday situations. In
total, nine participants took part, exceeding the minimum requirement of five
participants.

\section{Studying the Target User}

Dishify primarily targets regular home cooks who often need to decide what to
prepare using ingredients that are already available at home. This user group
was selected because it directly experiences the main problem addressed by the
application: deciding what to cook while considering available ingredients,
limited time, dietary preferences, and allergies.

The survey sample closely matched this profile. Four of the nine participants
reported cooking daily, while the remaining five cooked several times per week.
Before using Dishify, five participants identified ``not knowing what to make
with what I have'' as one of their main difficulties. Four participants also
reported that recipes often take too long to prepare. These findings show that
the selected participants experienced the exact problems Dishify was designed
to address.

Studying regular home cooks offered several advantages. They could evaluate
whether the recommendations were practical, whether the suggested recipes
matched everyday cooking situations, and whether the instructions and
ingredient substitutions were useful. Their previous cooking experience also
allowed them to assess whether the recommendations were realistic.

However, there were also limitations. Cooking habits, skills, and expectations
vary considerably between users. More experienced cooks may need less guidance,
while less experienced users may expect more detailed instructions. In
addition, the sample was relatively small and mainly included people who already
cook frequently. Therefore, the results provide useful initial evidence, but
they cannot yet be generalized to all possible users.

\section{User Survey Design}

The User Acceptance Testing was designed as a structured task-based test
followed by a survey. Each participant was asked to use Dishify as independently
as possible and complete the following process:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Open the Dishify application and review the available input options.
    \item Enter ingredients that could realistically be available in their kitchen.
    \item Specify any relevant dietary preferences or allergy restrictions.
    \item Request recipe recommendations.
    \item Review at least one recommended recipe.
    \item Examine the matched and missing ingredients, preparation information,
          and the explanation of why the recipe was recommended.
    \item Review any proposed ingredient substitutions.
    \item Decide whether the recommended recipe could realistically be prepared.
    \item Complete the post-test survey and provide optional written feedback.
\end{enumerate}

The survey evaluated seven main dimensions using a five-point Likert scale,
where 1 represented a very negative evaluation and 5 represented a very positive
evaluation:

\begin{itemize}
    \item overall ease of use;
    \item ease of obtaining a recommendation;
    \item clarity of the recommendation explanation;
    \item relevance of the recommended recipes;
    \item usefulness of ingredient substitutions;
    \item trust in the handling of allergies and dietary restrictions;
    \item likelihood of using Dishify again.
\end{itemize}

The survey also included questions about cooking frequency, current cooking
difficulties, positive aspects of Dishify, problems encountered during the test,
and desired future features. The combination of quantitative ratings and
open-ended feedback was chosen to provide both measurable results and more
detailed information about possible improvements.

\section{Performing the User Survey}

During the test, participants entered ingredients, requested a recommendation,
reviewed recipe information, and then completed the survey. The sessions focused
on whether users could complete the main recommendation process without
assistance and whether the results were perceived as useful, understandable,
relevant, and safe.

The collected data included cooking frequency, common problems experienced
before using Dishify, seven numerical ratings on a five-point scale, positive
aspects of the application, problems experienced during the test, and
suggestions for future improvements and features. No personally identifiable
information was required.

\section{Result Analysis and Evaluation}

The results were generally positive. All evaluated dimensions received an
average score above 3.5 out of 5.

    \centering

    \label{tab:uat-results}
    \begin{tabular}{@{}lr@{}}

        Overall ease of use                    & 4.67 \\

        Trust in allergy and dietary handling  & 4.44 \\
        Clarity of recommendation explanations & 4.11 \\
        Relevance of recommended recipes       & 4.00 \\
        Usefulness of ingredient substitutions & 3.88 \\
        Likelihood of using Dishify again       & 3.67 \\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}

Overall ease of use received the highest rating. This indicates that
participants generally understood the interface and were able to complete the
main workflow successfully. The lowest score was the likelihood of using
Dishify again. Although this result was still positive, it shows that good
usability does not automatically guarantee long-term use.

Participants identified several positive aspects of Dishify. The application
reduced decision fatigue by helping users decide what to cook. It also supported
the use of ingredients already available at home, which may reduce food waste.
In addition, the explanations increased transparency and helped users understand
why particular recipes were recommended.

The main problems identified were that recommendation generation was sometimes
too slow, ingredient matching could be too literal, cooking instructions needed
more detail, ingredient substitutions required clearer explanations, and
onboarding could be clearer.

The most requested future features were saving or bookmarking recipes, filtering
by cooking time, difficulty, and health goals, recipe photographs, and a
print-friendly recipe view.

Overall, the User Acceptance Testing indicates that Dishify addresses a relevant
problem for regular home cooks. Participants found the application easy to use,
trusted its handling of dietary restrictions, and valued its ingredient-based
recommendations. However, the lower score for future reuse shows that further
development should focus on speed, flexible ingredient matching, richer recipe
information, and features that encourage users to return.
